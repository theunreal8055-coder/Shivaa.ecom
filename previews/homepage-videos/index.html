Round 2 · The advanced set

Five more. Considerably bolder.

Same four films, same house rules (muted till tapped, poster-first, plays only while on screen) — but these five use the techniques behind today's most awarded websites: scroll-driven cinema, a watch-bezel dial, a living bento grid, a 3D viewing room, and a torch-lit vault.

⚠ Same deal as Round 1: the footage is studio-made stand-in film (AI) to preview the layouts — your four real 9:16 films drop straight in.
6
The film you walk through

The Scroll Cinema — scrollytelling

The screen holds still while the page scrolls through the four films — each one rising over the last with its chapter title, like turning the pages of a lookbook. A progress rail marks the chapters; a skip button respects your time.

Sits: a full-screen act between the hero banner and the category slider
SCROLL — THE FILMS PLAY THEMSELVES
What it is

A pinned, full-screen sequence: scrolling itself becomes the play button. Films crossfade with a slow settle-zoom, chapter titles rise, and only the film on screen is actually playing.

Why it works

This is the pattern behind Apple's product pages — the most cinematic storytelling the modern web has. Visitors don't watch your films; they move through them.

Watch-outs

It borrows the scroll for three or four swipes, so I ship a Skip button, and it quietly becomes a simple stacked section on old phones or reduced-motion settings.

7
The signature piece

The Chapter Ring — a dial of films

The film sits inside a slowly turning gold ring engraved with the house name — a ring, for a jeweller. Four nodes on the ring are the four films: tap one and the bezel turns, the film crossfades.

Sits: replaces the “Find your forever” section, or lives under the hero

What it is

A watch-bezel dial in gold: engraved rotating text, tick marks, four poster-set nodes — and the current film playing in the centre. The bezel gives a quarter-turn every time you change chapters.

Why it works

Nobody else will have this — it's a jewel itself, not just a video player. It reads instantly as “crafted”, which is exactly the house's promise.

Watch-outs

The rotating engraving stays decorative and faint so it never competes with the film. On phones the ring scales down but keeps the full mechanic.

8
The modern grid

The Living Bento — films + facts, one glance

One smart asymmetric grid — the “bento” style used by Apple and the best modern brands — mixing your four films with the house's truths: 22K, hallmark, today's gold rate, the 77 designs.

Sits: replaces the Bestsellers head area or sits right after the rates strip
What it is

A magazine-smart grid: one hero film large, three films woven between trust tiles — live gold rate with a shine sweep, “77 designs”, hallmark, a watch-all call to action. Hover any film: it breathes and offers play.

Why it works

The 2024–25 look: dense, confident, beautiful on phones. Films stop being decoration — they sit inside the sales argument, next to the numbers that close it.

Watch-outs

Only the tiles on screen play; each film still ships ≤2 MB. The rate tile would read your real live rate — numbers stay owner-locked, never invented.

9
The private viewing room

The 3D Showcase — depth you can feel

Four films standing on a shelf in real 3D perspective, reflections on a gold-lit floor, dust drifting. Move your cursor and the whole shelf leans with you; hover a film and it steps forward to meet you.

Sits: a full-width room after New Arrivals (desktop-first, phone-safe)
What it is

A genuine 3D scene — perspective, tilt, parallax on cursor, hover-to-approach, mirrored reflections and floating gold dust. On phones it gracefully flattens into a clean swipeable row.

Why it works

It feels like being handed the piece in a private viewing room — the most “expensive” impression of all ten ways, with barely any page weight (the 3D is pure CSS).

Watch-outs

3D and four films together is desktop-first by design; old or small phones automatically get the flat row, and reduced-motion settings still the drift.

10
The most interactive

The Spotlight Vault — a torch in the boutique

A near-black vault where the films stay dim until your cursor's warm light passes over them. Below, a tray that magnifies like a jeweller's loupe; tap a film and it zooms from its exact spot to full screen.

Sits: the closing act of the homepage, before the testimonials
Move your light

Everything shines in time

Glide your cursor (or thumb) across the vault — the light follows.

THE TRAY MAGNIFIES · TAP A FILM TO OPEN IT FULL-SCREEN
What it is

Three interactions in one: a spotlight that lights films as it passes, a magnifying tray of the four films, and a zoom transition that grows the exact tile you tapped into the full-screen reel.

Watch-outs

Dimmed films pause off-screen like everywhere else; on phones the light follows your thumb and dims gently when you lift it. Purely CSS + light math — no heavy library.

Why it works

It makes browsing feel physical — like walking the showroom at night with a torch. People remember pages they played with.
