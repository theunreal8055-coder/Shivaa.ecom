The offer · The rules · The date

The campaign in one breath

  • 12 Sep 2026 — compliant Instagram Live launch announcement + free pre-registration waitlist.
  • Qualifying order: a single paid, non-cancelled order containing ≥ 3 g gold and ≥ 100 g silver (verified by invoice weight at dispatch).
  • After purchase: a short skill/preference quiz → entry into the prize selection.
  • Free route: a genuine no-purchase entry with the same quiz & equal odds — this is what keeps the scheme a lawful contest, not a lottery.
  • Prize: one customer receives 100 g of 24K certified gold (refiner certificate; ~₹15 lakh current market value).
  • 31 Dec 2026 — CA-witnessed, live-streamed random draw on Instagram. Winner announced publicly. Entries close ~18–20 Dec.
Why compliant from day one
  • No-purchase free entry → removes "consideration" (lottery risk gone).
  • Real skill element in quiz → contest, not pure chance.
  • "Win a chance" copy (never "100% free") → CCPA/ASCI safe.
  • Winner's ~30% statutory TDS disclosed upfront (paid before delivery).
  • TN/WB excluded ("void where prohibited"); Maharashtra rules reviewed.
  • Written lawyer + CA opinions before entries open (gate register, file 03).

The moat · the page you asked for

Why other jewellers are NOT running this scheme

  • They can't do it legally. A purchase-only lucky draw is a lottery — most jewellers run them blind, without a free-entry route or lawyer T&C. We engineered the compliance first; a copycat without it inherits the CCPA/lottery exposure.
  • They won't risk margin. A ₹15L prize needs ~500–600 qualifying buyers on 4–8% margins. A typical jeweller won't put 6–10 months of profit behind one promotion.
  • They lack the inventory to absorb it. A ₹1L qualifying basket demands deep, varied stock across gold and silver — most shops hold ₹30–60L of slow stock, not a rotating ₹1.16 Cr engine.
  • Traffic wouldn't convert. The prize only pays if visitors buy. Without a 400,000-design catalogue + AI concierge, a giveaway sends traffic to a thin catalogue → wasted prize.
  • No tech stack. Entry ledger, audited draw tool, free-entry route, HUID verification, DPDP consent, queue handling — beyond a traditional jeweller's shop system.
  • Cash & liquidity. Prize + ads + stock upfront (~₹1.5 Cr) with a 3.5-month hold before the draw — most jewellers are inventory-cash-tight on exactly this scale.
  • Brand-optics fear. Heritage jewellers see "lucky draw" as cheap and damaging to trust; they quietly prefer plain discounts — leaving the high-attention game open.
  • Tax complexity. §128/§393(3) TDS on an in-kind prize, ITC reversal, PAN/KYC, notary draws — small firms avoid the paperwork entirely.
  • They can't fulfil 200 makers. Managing dispatch SLAs, hallmarking, returns and quality under one brand across 200 vendors is an operations machine most don't have.
  • And even if they copy it tomorrow — we hold first-mover data, supplier contracts, HUID-verified catalogue and the compliance playbook. The moat compounds with every customer we keep.
