## 2. Deltas and corrections (in priority order)

### D1. ✅ Resolved (owner, 9 Sep): there is no 12 September anchor — the "silent gap" question is moot
The plan's Phase 1 (12–30 Sep) is legal foundation only; public campaign launch is 1 Nov. Earlier docs had assumed a public Instagram Live announcement on 12 Sep — the owner has now confirmed that date was a mistake and is **withdrawn**. No teaser event is required on 12 Sep.

### D2. ⚠️ "Identical odds" free entry vs. "bonus entries for purchasers" — a real conflict with our confirmed hybrid
- File 04 (Section 2.3) requires the no-purchase route to have **identical odds of winning** — the standard that makes a sweepstake not-a-lottery.
- Our confirmed structure (file 01, Section 5.11 Option B) gave **bonus entries to purchasers** — which means purchasers would *not* have identical odds.
**Resolution (pick one for the lawyer to opine on):**
1. **Equal chances for everyone** (cleanest): purchasers earn **non-chance perks** — festive pricing, free gift packaging, hallmark certificate, priority dispatch, small instant cashback — but the same single entry as free entrants. Odds identical, consideration cleanly removed.
2. **Bonus entries, disclosed**: purchasers get extra entries (e.g., 1 purchase entry + 2 bonus). Many brands run this, but it partially re-introduces "more consideration → more chance", which is precisely the lottery element the free-entry fix removes. Only do this with the lawyer's **written** blessing and explicit odds disclosure.
**Recommendation: (1).** The campaign's purchase incentive shifts from "better odds" to "better deal" — which is also easier to defend as a trade practice.

### D3. ⚠️ Skill contest vs. sweepstake — the plan mixes two different legal models
File 04 Section 2.2 says winners should be decided **by quiz performance** (skill contest); Section 2.3 says **random draw with identical-odds free entry** (sweepstake). These are different compliance models with different paperwork. The public name "lucky draw" fits only the sweepstake; a skill contest shouldn't be described as a lucky draw at all.
**Decision to hand the lawyer:** primary model =
- **(i) Skill contest** — entry requires a genuinely scored quiz / closest-guess tie-breaker (the plan's MCX-closing-rate idea works here); winners by ranking; free-entry route optional; "lucky draw" wording dropped. **or**
- **(ii) Sweepstake** — random draw among all eligible entrants (quiz optional as a light gate/lead-qualifier); free route mandatory with equal odds; "lucky draw" wording OK. **or**
- **(iii) Hybrid (common in practice)** — eligible = quiz answered correctly (low skill bar, real pass mark) + random winner among eligible; free entry equal. FMCG-style. Lawyer to bless the exact wording.
**Recommendation: (iii)** — keeps your "quiz + lucky draw" story nearly as-is, with the skill bar making it a contest and the free route removing consideration.

### D5. Gross-up arithmetic (Scenario B) — one number to fix before the CA brief
File 04 computes Scenario B as ₹15,00,000 ÷ (1 − 30%) = **₹21,42,857**, ignoring cess. With effective rate ~31.2%, the true gross-up is **₹15,00,000 ÷ 0.688 ≈ ₹21,80,000**, i.e., ~**₹6.8L** extra cost (not ₹6.4L). Also note: Scenario A's TDS is ~₹4.68L at 31.2% (the plan says ₹4.5L at a flat 30%). Both are indicative — the CA's written model should use the exact cess rate for FY 2026-27 under the new Act.
