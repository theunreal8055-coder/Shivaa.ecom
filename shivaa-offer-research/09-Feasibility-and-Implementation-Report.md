## 1. Executive verdict (all six advisory roles)

1. **Commercial strategy (Business strategist + Digital/Instagram):** the concept is a coherent high-ticket customer-acquisition funnel. Its economics only work if it is treated as a **launch/CAC campaign**, not as a profitable offer on unit margin — at ~₹1.0–1.1L qualifying ticket and ~10–12% blended gross margin, break-even needs roughly **250–300 qualifying buyers (~₹3 Cr GMV)**, with the prize acting as the media budget. **[I]**
2. **Legal (Corporate + Consumer):** the scheme as originally phrased ("buy → pure-chance draw for ₹15L gold") is the **highest-risk shape** — a plausible illegal private lottery (prize + chance + consideration) and an unfair trade practice under the Consumer Protection Act 2019. The fix is standard and low-cost: **(a) a genuine free, no-purchase entry route with equal odds; (b) a real (scored) skill element in the quiz; (c) lawyer-drafted T&C; (d) an audited, live-streamed draw; (e) compliant advertising copy.** Without these, do not launch. **[I] + [R]**
3. **Tax (CA):** prize winnings attract a flat ~30% + 4% cess (≈31.2%) under the **Income-tax Act, 2025 §128** (TDS under **§393(3) Table Sl. No. 1**, formerly §115BB/§194B) — effective 1 Apr 2026, i.e., **in force for the 31 Dec 2026 draw**. In-kind prize ⇒ winner pays TDS before delivery (or gross-up). GST on the gifted biscuit needs an ITC-reversal ruling; the ECO-vs-inventory model changes GST/TCS duties. **[C]** + **[R]**
4. **Compliance (BIS/Metrology/Advertising/Privacy/AML):** gold-jewellery hallmarking (BIS + HUID) is mandatory; the **biscuit is a coin-format 24K product carrying a refiner's purity certificate** (not a jewellery hallmark); Legal Metrology packaging declarations apply; ad copy must follow CCPA Guidelines 2022 and ASCI; DPDP obligations are phased to 2026–27; as a dealer in precious metals Shivaa is a PMLA reporting entity (cash >₹10L triggers reports; digital model mostly outside cash thresholds). **[C]**
5. **The single most important scheduling finding:** with the current state (no lawyer/CA engaged, GSTIN not live, no T&C, no prize purchased), a **legally-clean full-scheme launch on 12 Sep 2026 is not achievable in three days**. Recommendation: **announce on 12 Sep** (honouring the date) with fully compliant wording and open **free pre-registration only**; open official entries once the legal gate register (file 03) is green — realistically early-mid October — and run the entry window to **~18–20 Dec**, with the winner live on **31 Dec 2026**. **[I]**

## 2. Legal analysis — corporate & commercial (Persona 1)

### 2.1 Entity and model decision: inventory ("seller of record") vs marketplace ("operator")

Shivaa's plan aggregates 200+ Mumbai manufacturers' designs on shivaa.in. The law treats two models very differently:

| Factor | Inventory model (Shivaa sells its own stock / buys from vendors) | Marketplace (vendors sell through shivaa.in) |
|---|---|---|
| Who is the seller to the consumer | Shivaa (all consumer liability, E-Commerce-Rules seller duties, GST on full sale) | Each vendor is the seller; Shivaa is an ECO (Section 52 TCS, GSTR-8, seller verification duties) |
| GST invoicing flow | Vendor → Shivaa (with ITC) → Customer | Vendor → Customer; Shivaa collects 1% TCS under **Section 52 CGST** [C] |
| Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 | Inventory entity: display legal name, CIN, GSTIN, address, grievance officer; publish returns/refunds etc. | Marketplace entity: + verify sellers (PAN/GSTIN), display seller details, written contracts with sellers, same grievance duties [C] |
| The 100g-biscuit campaign | Prize logistics handled wholly by Shivaa | Must ensure vendor-facing rules + consumer T&C consistent |

**Findings.** The repo and live site posture (own CMS, own catalogue, "seller of record", trust page with CIN/UDYAM) suggest an **inventory model**. That means: (i) **Section 52 GST TCS is not collected on Shivaa's own sales** — a third draft's claim that Shivaa must register as an ECO under §9(5) and collect 1% TCS on its own sales is wrong (see file 07); §9(5) concerns notified services, and §52 TCS applies only to supplies by registered third-party sellers. **[C]** (ii) All consumer liability for the 200 manufacturers' products sits with Shivaa — vendor agreements must shift/warranty that risk contractually (hallmark/HUID warranty, purity/weight, IP indemnity, defect liability, GSTIN+PAN on file), but Shivaa cannot contract away its statutory liability to the consumer. **[C]**
**Recommendation:** run the campaign on the **inventory model** for the qualifying/prize mechanics (single seller identity = single set of consumer-facing duties and cleaner prize T&C), and decide separately whether to evolve to true marketplace later. **[I]**

### 2.2 Vendor agreements & IP (200 manufacturers, 400,000+ designs)

- Written (templated) supply/listing agreement per vendor covering: licence to display/sell the designs and photos on shivaa.in; warranty of original design + non-infringement of third-party copyright/registered designs (Copyright Act 1957; Designs Act 2000); hallmark/HUID warranty (gold) and purity declarations (silver); weight/purity accuracy; dispatch SLA; returns/defect liability; **indemnity to Shivaa**; GSTIN/PAN/KYC. **[I]**
- **Do not list what cannot be delivered.** A 400,000-design claim that is not fulfil-able is itself a misleading-representation risk. Phase: launch with a verified festive set (2,000–10,000), scale weekly. **[I]**
- Never reproduce a manufacturer's product photos without authorisation — the site licence clause must cover photography/rendering rights at scale. **[C]**

### 2.3 Companies Act / governance

- Board resolution authorising: the offer, the ~₹15L prize + contingency, the ₹9L marketing budget, prize custodian and draw authority. **[I]**
- If the financier's money is a **loan**, document it under the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules / §73 so it is not mischaracterised as an unauthorised deposit. **[R]**

## 3. Consumer protection & consumer-court analysis (Persona 2)

### 3.1 The lottery analysis

- A "lottery" = **prize + chance + consideration**; the Lotteries (Regulation) Act, 1998 restricts lotteries to state governments; private lotteries are prohibited; **§294A IPC** penalises running lotteries (imprisonment exposure). **[C]**
- Purchase-required + pure chance + ₹15L prize plausibly satisfies all three elements. The quiz helps only if it is a **genuine skill element** (scored/judged), and even then the "contest of skill or chance to promote sale of goods" is expressly listed as an unfair trade practice under **CPA 2019 §2(47)** except as prescribed. **[C]**
- **The structural fix is the free-entry route:** a genuine "no purchase necessary" method of entry (as easy as buying, equal odds) removes "consideration", converting the draw to a promotional sweepstake/contest — the standard structure used by large Indian brands. **[I]** The brief's instinct that a *handwritten postcard* route is needed is a US-sweepstakes relic; an online/WhatsApp free form is the contemporary equivalent. **[I]**
- State-specific: **Tamil Nadu** prohibits purchase-linked prize schemes broadly (TN Prize Schemes (Prohibition) Act, 1979) → exclude TN residents in T&C ("void where prohibited"); **West Bengal** regulates prize competitions and needs licensing above thresholds → exclude or license; **Maharashtra** has its own lottery/prize-competition control/tax rules → confirm with counsel given Mumbai-manufacturer ties; **Rajasthan** (Shivaa's home state) public-gambling law → confirm scope. **[C] + [R]**
- The **Prize Chits & Money Circulation Schemes (Banning) Act, 1978** is **not** the operative frame (normal goods sale + promo ≠ chit), but keep the line in the T&C that goods are sold at genuine market price and nothing is paid "to enter the draw". **[I]**

### 3.2 "Free" claims, misleading ads & CCPA

- Claiming the biscuit is "**100% free**" while a purchase is required is a misleading-advertisement risk under **CCPA Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements, 2022** and CPA 2019; penalties up to **₹10L / 2 years (₹50L / 5 years on repeat)**; CCPA can order recall/withdrawal. **[C]**
- Required wording: "**Win a chance** to get a 100 g gold biscuit" / "one lucky winner receives…" — conditions stated prominently (not buried in fine print), value stated as "current market value ~₹15 lakh at [date], varies with the gold price". **[I]**
- Winner-paid statutory TDS must be disclosed in the same breath ("as required by law, ~30% TDS is deducted before delivery") — otherwise "100% free" complaints follow at handover. **[I]**

### 3.3 E-Commerce Rules 2020 & consumer-court exposure

- Display legal name, CIN, registered address, **GSTIN**, customer care + **grievance officer** (name/contact); acknowledge complaints ≤48 hrs, redress ≤30 days; publish returns/refunds/exchange/warranty/delivery/payment/charges. **[C]** Draft 2025 revisions and a BIS self-governance standard were under consultation as of 2026 — confirm current position with counsel. **[R]**
- Consumer Commissions (District ≤₹50L claims) are the likely forum for a ₹15L-prize dispute; exposure includes refund/compensation/costs plus CCPA *suo motu* action. **Defence kit:** audited entry ledger + SHA-256 freeze hash, independent CA/notary witness, unedited recording retained ≥8 years, published T&C, clean grievance log. **[I]**

## 4. Tax & accounting analysis (Persona 3 — CA)

### 4.1 Income tax on the prize — new Act sections (effective 1 Apr 2026)

- Winnings from lotteries/games/prize draws are taxed as casual income at a **flat 30% + 4% health & education cess ≈ 31.2%** (no exemptions/deductions/basic-exemption set-off). Old §115BB maps to **§128 of the Income-tax Act, 2025**; old §194B TDS maps to **§393(3) Table Sl. No. 1** (with a specific note for **in-kind prizes** where tax must be paid before release). **[C]** Confirm the exact charging/TDS section text with the CA (surcharge nuances differ for individuals vs companies). **[R]**
- FY 2026-27 is the first year under the new Act; the **31 Dec 2026 draw falls in that year**, so the new numbering applies. **[C]**
- TDS mechanics for the gold (in-kind): value at fair market value on draw date; winner pays ~31.2% cash before delivery (Scenario A — confirmed by owner) or Shivaa grosses up (Scenario B ≈ ₹15L ÷ 0.688 ≈ ₹21.8L, ~₹6.8L extra). Collect **PAN**; failure to furnish PAN raises the TDS rate (206AA-equivalent under the new Act — CA to confirm section). File returns; issue Form 16A/26AS entries. **[C] + [R]**

### 4.2 GST

- Gold/silver and jewellery: **3%** (1.5+1.5 or 3% IGST); making charges on jewellery are billed at 5% in practice — CBIC reads 3% on the retail transaction; confirm your invoicing with the CA. Coins/biscuits (HSN 7118/7108) also 3%. **[C]**
- The free biscuit: giving it without consideration is generally a **gift** ⇒ **ITC reversal under §17(5)(h)** (gifts >₹50k/recipient/year exceed the exception). A written CA model (marketing-expense-with-reversal vs bundled-supply treatment) is required **before purchasing the biscuit**. **[R]**
- E-commerce: inventory model ⇒ no §52 TCS on own sales; true marketplace later ⇒ ECO registration, §52 1% TCS (0.5+0.5 / 1% IGST), GSTR-8 by 10th. **[C]**
- e-invoicing threshold (B2B) is ₹5 Cr aggregate turnover; e-way bill rules for Chapter 71 jewellery have carve-outs (notably not mandatory for most Chapter 71 goods except in some states) — CA to confirm at campaign volumes. **[R]**

### 4.3 Valuation of the prize (~₹15L claim — verify)

Methodology: **valuation date** (recommend the **draw date** for TDS; the announcement date only for marketing copy) × **purity** (24K 999) × **reference rate** (IBJA / major-city 24K rate) × + 3% GST (if chargeable on the coin purchase) × + insurance/delivery (~0.1–0.2%) × making charges (≈ 0 for a biscuit).

| Component | Sep-2026 estimate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 24K gold | ₹1,49,420–1,55,290 per 10 g (Delhi/Mumbai, 8–9 Sep 2026) | City/source variance |
| 100 g 24K biscuit | **₹14.9 – 15.5 lakh** | +3% GST if applicable ⇒ ~₹15.4–16.0L delivered |
| Insurance/custody ~3 months | ₹10–25k | Insured, locked custody |
| Possible Dec rise (festive) | +5–10% | Budget ₹15–17L range |

**Finding:** "~₹15 lakh at today's rate" is fair as of Sep 2026; **never print a fixed rupee value**; state value as on the valuation date and use the **draw-date FMV for TDS**. **[I]**

### 4.4 Qualifying-purchase structure — AND / OR / basket (explicit analysis)

| Option | Ticket (Sep-26 rates) | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AND** — 5 g gold **and** 100 g silver in one paid order | ~₹1.0–1.1L | Single unambiguous trigger; serious-buyer filter; highest GMV per entry; simplest ledger validation & fraud checks; best odds clarity | Highest barrier → fewer entries | **✅ Recommended** |
| **OR** — 5 g gold **or** 100 g silver | ~₹25k (silver) – ₹75k (gold) | Lower barrier, more entries | Weak filter (₹25k silver-only orders flood in); diluted odds; two-tier complexity | ❌ |
| **Basket-equivalent** — any mix ≥ ~₹1L value | ~₹1.0L | Most flexible commercially | Valuation ambiguity (making charges, GST, live metal prices); gaming risk; disputes over "value" | ❌ (unless needed commercially, then only with real-time price engine + CA/lawyer input) |

**Recommended clause (least ambiguous):** "An 'Eligible Order' means a single paid, non-cancelled order placed on shivaa.in containing **at least 5 grams (gross weight) of gold jewellery/coins and at least 100 grams (gross weight) of silver jewellery/coins/artefacts**, each of declared BIS purity, paid in full and not refunded within the return window. Weight is verified from the invoice/dispatch record." **[I]**
Implementation: validate grams at order (cart logic) **and** re-verify at dispatch (invoice weights) to prevent cancellation/refund abuse; entry auto-issued after cooling-off. **[I]**

### 4.5 Accounting & cash-flow

- Prize + insurance booked as marketing/promotion expense (ITC-reversal treatment per §4.2); ₹9L ads deductible; TDS deposit is pass-through in Scenario A. **[I]**
- Vendor settlement 15/30/45-day terms improve cash cycle but only in a true marketplace or with careful working-capital management; do not present customer prepayments as revenue before fulfilment risk is assessed. **[I]**
- Cash sales ≥₹10L (rare in digital model) trigger **SFT Form 61A** reporting to the IT department; confirm thresholds. **[R]**

## 5. Compliance analysis (Persona 4)

### 5.1 BIS hallmarking & the prize biscuit

- **Gold jewellery** (14K/18K/22K/916): BIS hallmarking mandatory in notified districts; **HUID** per piece mandatory since 1 Apr 2023 (IS 1417/1418); selling non-hallmarked gold jewellery is an offence. Every Shivaa gold SKU needs a real HUID; the repo already has a HUID workflow — enforce per-SKU at listing and dispatch. **[C]**
- **Silver**: hallmarking **voluntary** under IS 2112:2025 (since 1 Sep 2025), under review for mandatory; treat BIS 925/999 marks as your quality standard for the campaign. **[C]**
- **The 100 g prize "biscuit"**: a 24K (999/995) coin/biscuit is not "jewellery" for hallmarking purposes; it should be bought from a **certified refiner (MMTC-PAMP / Government Mint type)** with a **purity certificate + invoice**, serial recorded, insured, custody-logged. (One external draft's claim that the biscuit must be "BIS-hallmarked 995/999 or criminal" is wrong for coins — see file 07; if you buy a BIS-hallmarked coin where available, that is a bonus, not a requirement.) **[I]**

### 5.2 Legal Metrology

- Packaged gold/silver items sold online must carry required declarations (net quantity by weight, purity, seller/manufacturer identity, price incl. GST) per the **Legal Metrology Act, 2009** and Packaged Commodities Rules; invoices must show exact weight, purity (e.g., 916/999), and making-charge/GST break-up. A Nov-2025 draft would add a searchable **country-of-origin filter** for imported goods on e-commerce platforms — monitor. **[C] + [R]**

### 5.3 Advertising & platform rules

- **CCPA/ASCI**: no "100% free" without conditions; material conditions prominent; "win a chance" framing; value stated as current-market, not guaranteed. **[C]**
- **Meta/Instagram** promotion rules: business/creator account; published official rules; the exact disclaimer *"This promotion is in no way sponsored, endorsed or administered by, or associated with, Instagram"*; no inaccurate tagging; paid influencers need #ad; keep entry records ≥90 days (we recommend ≥8 years with legal records). **[C]**

### 5.4 Privacy — DPDP Act 2023 (phased)

- Assent 11 Aug 2023; **Rules notified 13 Nov 2025**; Board constituted (13 Nov 2025); consent-manager provisions 13 Nov 2026; **substantive obligations (notice, consent, rights, retention, breach) fully effective 13 May 2027** — penalties from ~Nov 2026–May 2027. **[C]** For a Sep–Dec 2026 campaign, full penalty enforcement has not yet commenced, but design for compliance now (notice + consent at quiz/entry, retention limits, grievance/DPO contact, breach process). **[I]**

### 5.5 AML / PMLA

- Shivaa, as a **dealer in precious metals**, is a reporting entity under PMLA 2002 regardless of turnover; cash transactions ≥₹10L (single/aggregated monthly) → CTR to FIU-IND; suspicious transactions → STR; registration path via FIU-IND (direct >₹500 Cr turnover) or association. Digital order model is largely outside cash-reporting thresholds, but the AML/CFT programme and vendor KYC should exist. **[C] + [R]**

**Recommend AND**: "≥5 g gold AND ≥100 g silver in a single paid, non-cancelled order, verified at dispatch by invoice weights." Least ambiguous, best fraud-control, cleanest T&C and odds disclosure. As a Sep-2026 statement of current market value, yes (100 g 24K ≈ ₹14.9–15.5L + GST/delivery/insurance). It must not be a fixed promise; budget ₹15–17L; TDS computed on draw-date FMV. Methodology in §4.3. **[I]**

Run the campaign on the **inventory model** (Shivaa = seller of record) for cleaner liability and prize mechanics; onboard vendors with templated agreements (hallmark/HUID warranty, IP indemnity, GSTIN/KYC, SLA, defects); **phase listings** — advertise only what is fulfil-able; scale weekly; never claim 400k live until true. AI disclosure + human escalation; no prize/legal/purity promises by the bot; DPDP notice/consent; data-processor agreements; no AI in the draw; licence metadata/design use from vendors. (§6.) **[I]**

As the **draw/prize announcement**, yes — it is the single fixed public event and the plan's centrepiece. ### 9.3 The superseded 12 October 2026 date — possible interim role?
It can be an **interim milestone, not a winner-selection event**: e.g., a "campaign progress Live" (entries-so-far, catalogue highlights, first customer stories) or an optional small early-bird perk. Do not call it a "shortlist" announcement — shortlisting before a random draw confuses the model and raises consumer expectations (unfair-practice risk). If an early-bird prize is offered, keep it modest and TDS-compliant. **[I]**

### 9.4 The superseded "Bhai Dooj 11 November 2026" date — optional festive milestone?
Bhai Dooj 2026 falls **10–11 November** (most sources: 10 Nov). Use it as the **festive marketing peak** (with Dhanteras 6 Nov and Diwali 8 Nov) — the moment to push purchaser perks and entry momentum — not as any draw/selection event. It sits mid-campaign in this timeline, which is exactly where a sales spike belongs. **[C]/[I]**
